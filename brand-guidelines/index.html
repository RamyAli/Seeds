Brand guidelines · Edition 1.0 October 2026

Seeds, soil sensors and field tools for farmers. From seed to signal.

For designers, marketers, product teams, packaging suppliers and partners. Keep this book open when making anything that carries the Seed name.
01 · Brand

Who we are

Seed supplies the seed in the ground and the instruments that read the ground, so every farmer can make the next decision with better information.

Seed is an agricultural company with two halves that belong together. Seed Stock breeds and sells certified seed for field crops and vegetables. Seed Sense builds soil probes, weather nodes and the Seed Field app that turns their readings into plain advice: when to irrigate, when to fertilise, when to plant.

The brand has one job: make a farmer trust that what comes out of a Seed packet and what shows up on a Seed screen are equally reliable. Everything in this book serves that job.

Positioning

Mission

Put better information in every furrow.

Pair proven genetics with live soil data so growers spend less on water, fertiliser and guesswork.

Vision

Every field knows what it needs.

A future where a smallholder and a 5,000-hectare operation read their soil with the same clarity.

Promise

From seed to signal.

One company that stands behind both the seed you sow and the reading you act on.

Values

Grounded

We start from the field, not the lab. Every product is tested on working farms before launch.

Measured

We say what the data shows and how sure we are. Germination rates and sensor accuracy are printed, never hidden.

Patient

Farming runs on seasons. We build tools that last many of them and support them for years.

Shared

Growers own their field data. We help them share it with agronomists and co-ops on their terms.

Who we talk to

Growers

Family farms to large operations. Practical, time-poor, sceptical of hype. They want yields, not dashboards.

Agronomists & advisers

They want the numbers, the methods behind them and export formats that fit their tools.

Distributors & co-ops

They need clear packaging, reliable supply and a brand their members already recognise.

02 · Voice

How we sound

We write the way a good agronomist talks at the edge of a field: plainly, with numbers when they help, and with respect for the grower's experience. We never explain farming to farmers.

Plain-spoken

Short sentences. Common words. One idea at a time.

Jargon-heavy, buzzword-driven or "revolutionary".

Precise

Real units, real ranges: 68 days to maturity, ±2% moisture accuracy.

Vague promises like "boost your yields".

Warm

Encouraging, on the grower's side, honest about a bad season.

Cute, folksy or full of farm puns.

Calm

Alerts state the problem and the next step.

Alarmist. No red capitals, no exclamation marks in warnings.

03 · Logo

The Seed logo

The logo is the name Seed, set in a high-contrast serif and held between two solid brackets. The brackets are the husk: the protective coat around a seed. Together they read as something valuable, protected and about to grow.

Always use the master artwork files. Never retype the name, redraw the brackets or rebuild the lockup.

Primary logo · Seed Orange on Loam

Anatomy

  1. 1

    Husk brackets

    Two solid blocks with a concave inner edge. They are exactly as tall as the capital S, and they mirror each other. Never use one without the other.

  2. 2

    Wordmark

    "Seed" in a high-contrast display serif. The open counters of the e's and the tall stem of the d echo a seed coat splitting and a shoot rising.

  3. x

    Unit of measure

    The width of one bracket is x. Clear space and the mark's spacing are all measured in x.

Clear space

Keep at least 1x of empty space on every side of the logo, where x is the width of one bracket. Nothing else, not text, edges or other logos, enters this zone. On packaging and signage, use 2x where room allows.

Minimum size

Below these sizes the hairlines of the serif break up. When space is tighter, switch to the mark.

Logo family

Primary logo

The default. Use it whenever there is room: packaging fronts, website header, signage, presentations.

Mark

The S held in the husk. For device housings, social avatars, favicons and anywhere the full logo would fall below 120 px.

App icon

The mark on a Loam tile, scaled to 60% of the tile width. Used for Seed Field and on-device screens.

Colour versions

Seed Orange on Loam is the hero pairing. On light grounds, the logo goes Loam. Seed Orange is not used for the logo on Silt or white, because it falls below 3:1 contrast there.

Misuse

These are the mistakes we see most. If a layout seems to need one of them, change the layout instead.

Don't stretch or squash. Scale proportionally only.

Don't recolour. Only the approved colour versions above.

Don't move the brackets. Their spacing is fixed.

Don't rotate. The logo always sits level.

Don't use orange on light grounds. Use the Loam version.

Don't outline or add effects. No strokes, shadows or gradients.

05 · Typography

Typography

The logo is drawn from New York, a serif that is licensed only for Apple platforms. For everything we set ourselves, we use three free, open-licence families from Google Fonts: a serif that shares New York's proportions, a sturdy grotesque for reading, and a mono for numbers and specifications.

Display

Newsreader

Headlines, pull quotes, product and variety names. Light (300) and Regular (400), with italics for emphasis. Use the optical-size axis: large sizes get the finer display cut.

Rules

  • Headlines are sentence case. No all-caps headlines; caps are for small mono labels only.
  • Never set body copy in Newsreader below 18 px, or headlines in Schibsted Grotesk above 36 px.
  • Every number a grower acts on gets its unit: 31.6% VWC, 18.4 °C, 68 days.
  • Left-align text. Centre only short lines on packaging and covers.
  • Keep body text between 55 and 75 characters per line.
06 · Graphics & icons

Graphic language

Two devices come straight from the logo, and one from the field. Use one of them per layout, not all three.

92%germination rate

Husk brackets

The logo's brackets, used on their own to hold the single most important number or word in a layout. One pair per page, in Seed Orange.

Furrow lines

Evenly spaced curved lines, like ploughed rows seen across a field. Loam on Silt or Silt on Loam, 1–1.5 px. Seed Orange marks a point of interest on a row.

Seed spacing grid

A grid of seed shapes at planting spacing. Use as a quiet background on Loam; highlight a group in Seed Orange to show a plot or sensor zone.

Icons

Line icons on a 24 px grid with a 1.5 px stroke, round caps and joins. Icons are drawn in Loam or Silt. Seed Orange is reserved for an active or selected state.

07 · Photography

Photography

Our photographs show real growers on real farms, in working conditions. Light is natural and low: early morning or the last two hours before sunset, which is where Seed Orange comes from. Colour is warm and slightly muted, never over-saturated.

Hands & soil · 4:5

Close to the ground

Hands in soil, seed in a palm, a probe going in. Shallow depth of field, eye level with the crop.

Seed detail · 4:5

The seed itself

Macro shots of seed on dark soil or Loam backdrops. Treat seed like a precious object.

Field · 4:5 or 16:9

The working field

Wide shots with real rows, real machinery, real weather. Horizon in the upper third.

Do

  • Photograph customers and staff on their own farms, with permission.
  • Show the product in use, with soil on it.
  • Include different regions, crops, ages and farm sizes.
  • Leave calm space for a headline.

Don't

  • Use posed stock photos of spotless farmers in new clothes.
  • Use glowing "digital farm" composites or floating data overlays.
  • Push saturation or add heavy filters.
  • Place the logo over a busy part of a photo.
